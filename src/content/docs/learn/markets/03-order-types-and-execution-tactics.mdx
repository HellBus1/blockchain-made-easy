In Track 3, we examined basic Limit and Market orders. While sufficient for simple accumulation, relying exclusively on simple orders leaves you vulnerable to the 24/7 nature of cryptocurrency markets.

Crypto never sleeps. If a major regulatory announcement or liquidation cascade occurs while you are sleeping at 3:00 AM, you cannot wake up in time to manually sell your assets.

Professional traders use **Conditional and Bracket Orders** to automate both profit-taking and loss prevention with mathematical discipline.

---

### 1. The Stop-Limit Order

A **Stop-Limit Order** is a two-step conditional trigger:

```
STOP-LIMIT WORKFLOW:
  Market Price drops to $65,000 (TRIGGER PRICE) ──► Submits Limit Sell at $64,800 (LIMIT PRICE)
```

- **Stop Price (Trigger)**: The threshold that wakes up the exchange matching engine and submits your order.
- **Limit Price (Execution)**: The worst price you are willing to accept for the fill.

<Aside type="tip" title="Execution Pro Tip">
Never set your Stop Price and Limit Price to the exact same number! During a violent flash crash, the price might gap directly through your price level, leaving your order unfilled while the market plunges further. Always set your Limit Price slightly lower (e.g. 0.3%–0.5% below the Stop trigger) to guarantee immediate execution.
</Aside>

### 2. OCO (One-Cancels-the-Other): The Professional Bracket

The most important order type for stress-free crypto trading is the **<Term id="oco-order">OCO (One-Cancels-the-Other)</Term>** order.

An OCO bundles two orders simultaneously:
1. A **Take-Profit Limit Order** (above current price).
2. A **Stop-Loss Stop-Limit Order** (below current price).

```
OCO DUAL-BRACKET EXECUTION:
                               ┌──► TAKE-PROFIT LIMIT: $75,000 (Hits target! Sells for +$1,000 profit)
                               │    (Automatically cancels Stop-Loss below!)
  Current Price: $68,000 ──────┤
                               │    (Automatically cancels Take-Profit above!)
                               └──► STOP-LOSS TRIGGER: $66,000 (Hits stop! Sells for -$100 max risk)
```

**Why is OCO essential?**
On most exchanges, you cannot place a separate Limit Sell and Stop-Loss on the same coins because your balance is locked by the first order. 

An OCO solves this by linking both instructions: **the second one of them is triggered, the matching engine automatically cancels the other!** You can close your laptop, go to sleep, and know your capital is 100% protected.

### 3. Trailing Stop-Loss: Riding Winning Trends

When you enter a trade that surges higher, how do you decide when to take profits? If you exit too early, you miss an explosive multi-week bull run. If you hold too long, the market reverses and wipes out your gains.

A **Trailing Stop-Loss** solves this dilemma by dynamically following the price upward:

```
TRAILING STOP (SET AT 5% TRAIL):
  - Entry Price: $60,000 ──► Stop-Loss is set at $57,000 (-5%)
  - Price rallies to $70,000 ──► Stop-Loss automatically ratchets UP to $66,500 (-5% from peak)
  - Price rallies to $80,000 ──► Stop-Loss automatically ratchets UP to $76,000 (-5% from peak)
  - Price drops from $80,000 to $76,000 ──► STOP TRIGGERED!
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Result: You banked +$16,000 in net profit (+26.6%) without guessing the top!
```

Crucially, **the trailing stop only moves in one direction: UP**. If the market pulls back, the stop-loss level remains locked at the highest peak reached, protecting your accumulated fiat earnings.

### Execution Flags: Post-Only & Time-in-Force

When executing high-capital trades, use these exchange flags to maximize your fill quality:

1. **Post-Only (Maker Guarantee)**: Ensures your limit order is posted to the order book as a Maker order. If the order would execute immediately as a Taker (costing higher fees), the system rejects it automatically.
2. **GTC (Good-Til-Canceled)**: The default setting. Your order stays active on the books indefinitely until filled or manually canceled.
3. **IOC (Immediate-Or-Cancel)**: Fills as much of the order as possible immediately at the specified price and cancels any remaining unfilled balance.
4. **FOK (Fill-Or-Kill)**: The order must be filled in its entirety immediately, or the entire order is canceled without partial fills.
