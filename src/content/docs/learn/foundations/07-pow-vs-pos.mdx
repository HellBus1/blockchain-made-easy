In a village with 10,000 notebooks, who gets the authority to write down the official page of transactions and seal it?

If anyone could do it for free, an attacker could create a million fake computers (a **Sybil Attack**) and flood the network with fraudulent pages. To prevent this, the protocol demands that sealing a page must **cost something real**.

## Comparison: The Two Pillars of Consensus

| Attribute                | Proof of Work (PoW)                                                  | Proof of Stake (PoS)                                                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mechanism**            | Racing to solve ultra-hard cryptographic math puzzles.               | Locking up existing digital assets as a validator security deposit.   |
| **Real-World Metaphor**  | Burning physical energy and computational power to prove commitment. | Placing money in a locked escrow vault to prove honesty.              |
| **Penalty for Cheating** | Wasted electricity bills and depreciating specialized hardware.      | Security deposit is permanently destroyed or slashed by the protocol. |
| **Real-World Networks**  | Bitcoin (BTC), Litecoin (LTC)                                        | Ethereum (ETH), Solana (SOL), Cardano (ADA)                           |

## Proof of Work: The Mathematical Nonce Search

In Bitcoin, miners gather pending transactions and combine them with an arbitrary 32-bit number called a **Nonce** (number used once):

```text
SHA-256(Block Data + Nonce) < Target Difficulty
```

Because hash outputs are completely random, there is no formula to predict which nonce will work. The only way to find it is brute-force trial and error—guessing quintillions of numbers every second.

When a miner finally discovers a winning nonce:

1. They broadcast the block and the winning nonce to the world.
2. Every other node can verify the solution in **less than a millisecond** by running SHA-256 once.
3. The miner earns newly minted coins: **The Block Reward**.
