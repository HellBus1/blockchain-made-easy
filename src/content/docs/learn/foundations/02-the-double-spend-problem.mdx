Physical items have a built-in law of physics: if you hand someone a paper Rp 50,000 banknote or an apple, you no longer possess it. You cannot hand that exact same apple to two different people at the exact same moment.

Digital files, on the other hand, are just streams of ones and zeros (`1`s and `0`s). When you email an image or a PDF document to a coworker, you don't lose the file; your computer creates a **perfect clone** and transmits the copy.

---

## The Fatal Flaw of Pure Digital Cash

Imagine if digital money worked like ordinary computer files (like an MP3 or text file):

```
       ┌───────────────────────────────┐
       │   User holds 1 Digital Coin   │
       └──────────────┬────────────────┘
                      │
        ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
        ▼                           ▼
[Copies file to Recipient A]   [Copies file to Recipient B]
        ▼                           ▼
Bought Dinner (Rp 100,000)     Bought Shoes (Rp 100,000)
```

Without a central authority overseeing the <Term id="ledger">ledger</Term>, an unscrupulous actor could spend the exact same 100,000 Rupiah twice. This is known as the <Term id="double-spend">Double-Spend Problem</Term>.

---

## Why We Trusted the Bank

To stop this fraud, society instituted a trusted middleman: a **Bank**.

When Alice wants to transfer Rp 100,000 to Bob:

1. Alice sends a transaction request to the bank.
2. The bank verifies Alice's account has at least Rp 100,000.
3. If Alice simultaneously sends a request to pay Charlie with the same money, the bank's database processes the first transaction, docks Alice's balance, and rejects the second transaction with `Insufficient Funds`.

<Aside type="caution" title="The Single Point of Failure">
  The central bank acts as a firewall against double-spending. But this introduces massive
  structural trade-offs:
  - **Centralized Vulnerability**: If the bank's central database goes offline or is hacked, commerce halts.
  - **Custody Risk**: You do not control your money directly; you control an IOU against the bank.
  - **Toll Booth**: The institution charges transaction fees, spreads, and can freeze accounts without judicial process.
</Aside>

---

## The Holy Grail: Trustless Consensus

For decades, computer scientists and cypherpunks tried to build digital money without a middleman. Every attempt failed because without a centralized server, who decides which transaction came first? If Alice broadcasts one payment to Europe and another to Asia at the exact same microsecond, which one is real?

In 2008, an anonymous programmer using the pseudonym **Satoshi Nakamoto** published the Bitcoin whitepaper, solving this problem once and for all with a concept called **The Shared Notebook**.
