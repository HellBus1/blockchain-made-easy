When you post a video on Instagram or TikTok, your phone uploads the video to a central server (Meta or ByteDance), which then distributes it to anyone who follows you.

In a decentralized blockchain, there is no central headquarters. Instead, the network relies on one of nature's most resilient communication models: <Term id="gossip-protocol">The Gossip Protocol</Term>.

## The Three Steps of Gossip Propagation

```
 1. BROADCAST                   2. VERIFICATION                   3. GOSSIP EXPANSION
Alice transmits message ──► Node checks its local ──► Verified message gossiped to 8 peers.
to nearest node:             history book to verify    Those 8 tell 8 more (64 peers).
"Alice sends 1 BTC to Bob"   Alice owns 1 BTC.         Within seconds, network is synced!
```

1. **Broadcast**: Your wallet app connects to the nearest internet <Term id="node">node</Term> and transmits the signed transaction message: `Alice sends 1 Coin to Bob`.
2. **Local Verification**: That receiving node doesn't blindly trust you. It opens its local copy of the historical <Term id="ledger">ledger</Term> and runs mathematical checks:
   - Does Alice possess the coin?
   - Does the signature match Alice's public address?
3. **Gossip Fanout**: Once verified, the node forwards ("gossips") the message to **8 connected peers**. Each of those 8 nodes verifies the transaction independently and tells 8 more peers.

<Aside type="tip" title="Exponential Speed">
  Through geometric progression ($8 \times 8 \times 8 \times 8...$), a message can reach 10,000+
  computers located in dozens of countries in less than 3 to 5 seconds.
</Aside>

## Defeating the Double-Spend Race

Suppose Alice is malicious and tries to pull off a race attack:

- She sends `Alice pays Bob 1 BTC` to a node in Singapore.
- At the exact same second, she sends `Alice pays Charlie 1 BTC` to a node in London.

Whichever message reaches each node first is marked as pending in its <Term id="mempool">mempool</Term>. When the second transaction arrives, the node sees that the exact same coin has already been earmarked in its pending memory pool, and **drops the conflicting duplicate on the spot**.
