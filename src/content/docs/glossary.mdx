### A–E

#### Avalanche Effect

A property of cryptographic hash functions where even a tiny alteration in the input data (such as changing one letter or capitalization) results in a drastically different, seemingly random output hash.

#### Blockchain

A distributed, decentralized, and append-only ledger composed of blocks linked together using cryptographic hashes. Once sealed, historical blocks cannot be altered without invalidating all subsequent blocks.

#### Block Reward

The protocol incentive paid to a miner or validator who successfully seals a new block. Consists of newly minted cryptocurrency plus accumulated transaction fees.

#### Custodial vs. Non-Custodial

A custodial service (like a centralized exchange) holds your private keys on your behalf. A non-custodial wallet (self-custody) grants you sole possession of the private key or seed phrase: _"Not your keys, not your coins."_

#### Double-Spend Problem

The vulnerability in digital cash where an asset can be duplicated and spent more than once before verification. Blockchains solve this without a central bank by synchronizing an immutable shared ledger across thousands of nodes.

### F–M

#### Fiat Money

Government-issued legal tender not backed by a physical commodity like gold (e.g., Indonesian Rupiah [IDR], US Dollar [USD], Euro [EUR]).

#### Gossip Protocol

A peer-to-peer communication protocol where nodes randomly share new transactions and blocks with their nearest peers (typically 8 connected peers), rapidly synchronizing the global network within seconds.

#### Halving

A programmed protocol event in Bitcoin occurring every 210,000 blocks (roughly every 4 years) where the rate of new coin issuance is halved (e.g., from 50 BTC down to 25, 12.5, 6.25, and 3.125 BTC).

#### Hash (Cryptographic)

A mathematical one-way function that compresses any input data of arbitrary size into a fixed-length string of characters (e.g., 64 hexadecimal characters in SHA-256).

---

### N–Z

#### Node

An independent computer running the blockchain software client that maintains a copy of the ledger, verifies transactions, and enforces network protocol rules.

#### Proof of Stake (PoS)

A consensus mechanism where validators lock up a deposit of cryptocurrency (stake) to earn the right to propose and validate blocks. Cheating or submitting malicious blocks results in the validator's stake being slashed (burned).

#### Proof of Work (PoW)

A consensus mechanism requiring miners to expend physical electrical energy and compute cycles to solve a challenging cryptographic puzzle before sealing a block.

#### Public Key & Private Key

A cryptographic keypair. The **Public Key** (derived into your wallet address) is safe to share with the world like an account number or glass mailbox. The **Private Key** (or 12-word seed phrase) is your secret digital signature used to authorize transfers.
